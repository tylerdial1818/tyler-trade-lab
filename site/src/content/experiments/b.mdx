## Question

Two questions about one weekly number. First, can information that is public before 8:30 a.m. Eastern on Thursday forecast initial jobless claims better than the Kalshi price? Second, when the number comes out, how quickly does the price move, and does it move far enough?

## Price

The benchmark is the market midpoint 60 minutes before the release.

## Reason

Layoffs happen before people file claims, and some are announced in advance through state layoff notices. If the market does not use that information, a forecast that does should be more accurate.

## Timing

The outcome is the first number the Department of Labor reports, not the revised figure published a week later. Every input is dated by when it became public. See [Two kinds of uncertainty](../../essays/two-kinds-of-uncertainty/) for why some of the error will remain whatever data is added.

## Mechanism

The release time is fixed weeks in advance. That makes the second question a clean event study: the timing of the news is not caused by anything happening in the market, so the price change can be compared with the size of the surprise. Thursdays without a release serve as placebo dates.

## Forecast

Four forecasts: the market price, a simple model using last week's number and seasonal patterns, the same model with layoff notices added, and a regularized model with the same inputs.

## Test

The full specification is in [the pre-registration](https://github.com/tylerdial1818/tyler-trade-lab/blob/main/specs/B-jobless-claims.yaml). With about 52 releases a year, the test can detect a large forecasting improvement or a clear price response. It cannot support any claim about trading profit, and it does not make one.

## Result

Not yet run.

## Limitations

The price response needs order books recorded minute by minute around each release. That recording starts when this experiment starts, so the response result will grow one week at a time.
